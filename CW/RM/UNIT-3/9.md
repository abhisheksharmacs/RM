# Items (or Statements) in a Questionnaire

In research, **items (or statements)** are the individual questions or statements presented to respondents to measure their attitudes, opinions, perceptions, or behaviors.

They are especially important in **Likert Scales** and other attitude measurement scales.

---

## Definition

An **item** is a single statement on which respondents indicate their level of agreement, disagreement, frequency, importance, or satisfaction.

### Example

**Research Topic:** Online Shopping Satisfaction

**Item 1:** "Online shopping saves me time."

Response:

* Strongly Agree
* Agree
* Neutral
* Disagree
* Strongly Disagree

Here, the statement **"Online shopping saves me time"** is called an  **item** .

---

## Examples of Items

### Customer Satisfaction Study

1. Online shopping saves me time.
2. Product descriptions are accurate.
3. Delivery services are reliable.
4. Online payments are secure.
5. Customer support resolves issues quickly.

Each statement is an  **item** .

---

## Characteristics of Good Items

### 1. Clear

✅ "The website is easy to use."

❌ "The website has a multifaceted interface."

---

### 2. Simple

✅ "I find online shopping convenient."

❌ "The omnichannel purchasing process improves consumer experience."

---

### 3. Specific

✅ "Products are delivered on time."

❌ "The service is good."

---

### 4. Relevant

The item should relate directly to the research objective.

---

### 5. Unbiased

✅ "How satisfied are you with delivery services?"

❌ "Don't you think the delivery service is excellent?"

---

## Positive and Negative Items

### Positive Item

"Online shopping is convenient."

* Strongly Agree = 5
* Strongly Disagree = 1

### Negative Item

"Online shopping is a waste of time."

* Strongly Agree = 1
* Strongly Disagree = 5

Negative items are often included to reduce response bias.

---

## Example of a Likert Scale with Multiple Items

**Measure: Customer Satisfaction**

| Item                             | Response    |
| -------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Online shopping saves time.      | SA A N D SD |
| Products are delivered on time.  | SA A N D SD |
| Online payment is secure.        | SA A N D SD |
| Customer support is helpful.     | SA A N D SD |
| The website is easy to navigate. | SA A N D SD |

The collection of these items forms a  **scale** .

---

## Item vs Scale

| Item                                      | Scale                                    |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Single statement/question                 | Collection of items                      |
| Measures one aspect                       | Measures a complete construct            |
| Example: "Online shopping is convenient." | Customer Satisfaction Scale (5-10 items) |

---

## Practical Example

### Construct: Student Satisfaction with Online Learning

**Items:**

1. Online classes are easy to access.
2. Learning materials are useful.
3. Teachers explain concepts clearly.
4. Online assessments are fair.
5. Technical support is adequate.

Together, these items measure the construct  **Student Satisfaction** .

---

## Exam Answer (5 Marks)

**Items or statements are individual questions or declarative sentences used in a questionnaire to measure respondents' attitudes, opinions, perceptions, or behaviors. A set of related items forms a scale. Good items should be clear, simple, specific, relevant, and unbiased. For example, in a customer satisfaction survey, statements such as "The website is easy to use" and "Products are delivered on time" are considered questionnaire items.**

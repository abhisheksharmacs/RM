# Comparative and Non-Comparative Scales

In marketing and research, attitude measurement scales are broadly classified into:

1. **Comparative Scales**
2. **Non-Comparative Scales**

---

# 1. Comparative Scales

A **comparative scale** requires respondents to compare one object, brand, product, or service with another.

### Key Feature

✅ Comparison is mandatory.

### Example

**Which online shopping platform do you prefer?**

* Amazon
* Flipkart

The respondent must choose one by comparing both.

---

## Types of Comparative Scales

### A. Paired Comparison Scale

Respondents choose one option from a pair.

**Example**

Which smartphone brand do you prefer?

* Apple
* Samsung

✅ Simple comparison between two alternatives.

---

### B. Rank Order Scale

Respondents rank several items according to their preference.

**Example**

Rank the following apps from most preferred (1) to least preferred (4):

* Amazon ___
* Flipkart ___
* Myntra ___
* Meesho ___

✅ Shows preference order.

---

### C. Constant Sum Scale

Respondents distribute a fixed number of points among options.

**Example**

Allocate 100 points according to importance:

| Factor         | Points |
| -------------- | ------ |
| Price          | 40     |
| Quality        | 30     |
| Delivery Speed | 20     |
| Brand Name     | 10     |
| Total          | 100    |

✅ Reveals relative importance.

---

### D. Q-Sort Scale

Respondents sort items into categories based on preference.

**Example**

Sort brands into:

* Most Preferred
* Preferred
* Neutral
* Least Preferred

✅ Frequently used in psychological and marketing studies.

---

# 2. Non-Comparative Scales

A **non-comparative scale** evaluates only one object at a time.

### Key Feature

✅ No comparison required.

### Example

**Rate Amazon's delivery service.**

* Excellent
* Good
* Average
* Poor

The respondent evaluates Amazon alone.

---

## Types of Non-Comparative Scales

### A. Continuous Rating Scale

Respondents mark a position on a continuous line.

**Example**

Poor ───────────── Excellent

✅ Measures intensity of feeling.

---

### B. Itemized Rating Scale

Provides specific response categories.

---

#### (i) Likert Scale

**Amazon provides reliable service.**

* Strongly Agree
* Agree
* Neutral
* Disagree
* Strongly Disagree

---

#### (ii) Semantic Differential Scale

**Website Design**

Poor ◻ ◻ ◻ ◻ ◻ Excellent

---

#### (iii) Stapel Scale

Customer Service

+3

+2

+1

Service Quality

−1

−2

−3

−4

−5

---

# Comparative vs Non-Comparative Scales

| Basis         | Comparative Scale            | Non-Comparative Scale             |
| ------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Meaning       | Compares two or more objects | Evaluates a single object         |
| Focus         | Relative preference          | Absolute evaluation               |
| Example       | Amazon vs Flipkart           | Rate Amazon only                  |
| Data Obtained | Relative ranking             | Individual ratings                |
| Complexity    | Higher                       | Simpler                           |
| Accuracy      | Good for preference analysis | Good for satisfaction measurement |

---

# 5-Mark Exam Answer

**Comparative scales require respondents to compare two or more objects and express their preferences. Examples include Paired Comparison, Rank Order, Constant Sum, and Q-Sort scales. Non-comparative scales evaluate a single object independently without comparing it to others. Examples include Likert Scale, Semantic Differential Scale, Stapel Scale, and Continuous Rating Scale. Comparative scales measure relative preferences, while non-comparative scales measure absolute attitudes and perceptions.** ✅

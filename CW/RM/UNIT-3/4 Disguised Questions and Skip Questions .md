# Disguised Questions and Skip Questions

## 1. Disguised Questions

### Definition

A **disguised question** is a question in which the actual purpose of the question is hidden from the respondent. It is used when direct questioning may lead to biased, inaccurate, or socially desirable responses.

### Purpose

* To reduce respondent bias.
* To obtain more honest answers.
* To study sensitive attitudes, beliefs, or behaviors indirectly.

### Examples

**Direct Question:**

> Do you frequently waste time on social media?

**Disguised Question:**

> How do people of your age group generally use social media during their free time?

Another example:

Instead of asking:

> Do you prefer expensive brands because of social status?

Ask:

> What factors do people usually consider when purchasing premium brands?

### Advantages

* Reduces social desirability bias.
* Encourages honest responses.
* Useful for sensitive topics.

### Disadvantages

* More difficult to design.
* Responses may be harder to interpret.
* Respondents may misunderstand the purpose.

## 2. Skip Questions (Contingency Questions)

### Definition

A **skip question** is a question that directs respondents to different subsequent questions based on their previous answers.

### Purpose

* Avoids asking irrelevant questions.
* Reduces questionnaire length.
* Improves respondent experience.
* Increases data quality.

### Example

**Q1. Do you own a car?**

* □ Yes → Go to Q2
* □ No → Skip to Q5

**Q2. How often do you drive your car?**

* Daily
* Weekly
* Occasionally

Here, respondents who do not own a car skip the car-related questions.

### Another Example

**Q1. Have you ever taken an online course?**

* Yes → Continue to Q2
* No → Skip to Q4

### Advantages

* Saves time.
* Prevents irrelevant responses.
* Makes surveys more efficient.

### Disadvantages

* Poor design can confuse respondents.
* Errors in skip logic may lead to missing data.

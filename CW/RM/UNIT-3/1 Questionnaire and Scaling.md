### Questionnaire and Scaling

**Questionnaire** and **Scaling** are important tools used in research to collect data from respondents.

---

## 1. Questionnaire

### Definition

A **questionnaire** is a structured set of questions designed to gather information from respondents about their opinions, attitudes, behaviors, or experiences.

### Purpose

* Collect primary data
* Understand respondents' views and preferences
* Conduct surveys efficiently

### Types of Questions

#### a) Open-ended Questions

Respondents answer in their own words.

**Example:**

> What challenges do you face while attending online classes?

#### b) Closed-ended Questions

Respondents choose from given options.

**Example:**

> Do you use online learning platforms regularly?

* Yes
* No

### Advantages

* Easy to administer
* Cost-effective
* Can reach many respondents
* Provides standardized data

### Example

**Research Topic:** Impact of Social Media on Students

Questionnaire:

1. How many hours do you spend on social media daily?
2. Which platform do you use most frequently?
3. Do you think social media affects your academic performance?
   * Yes
   * No

---

## 2. Scaling

### Definition

**Scaling** is a technique used to measure attitudes, opinions, perceptions, or behaviors by assigning numbers or categories to responses.

### Purpose

* Quantify qualitative data
* Measure intensity of feelings or opinions
* Facilitate statistical analysis

### Example

Instead of asking:

> "Are you satisfied with online learning?"

Scaling measures the degree of satisfaction.

---

## Common Types of Scales

### 1. Likert Scale

Most commonly used scale in research.

**Example:**

**Online learning improves my understanding of subjects.**

| Response          | Score |
| ----------------- | ----- |
| Strongly Agree    | 5     |
| Agree             | 4     |
| Neutral           | 3     |
| Disagree          | 2     |
| Strongly Disagree | 1     |

---

### 2. Rating Scale

Respondents rate something on a numerical scale.

**Example:**

Rate your satisfaction with online classes:

1 ⭐ → Very Poor

5 ⭐ → Excellent

---

### 3. Semantic Differential Scale

Measures attitudes between two opposite adjectives.

**Example:**

Online Learning:

Poor ⬜ ⬜ ⬜ ⬜ ⬜ Excellent

Traditional ⬜ ⬜ ⬜ ⬜ ⬜ Modern

---

### 4. Ranking Scale

Respondents rank items according to preference.

**Example:**

Rank the following social media platforms:

___ Instagram

___ YouTube

___ Facebook

___ Twitter

---

## Difference Between Questionnaire and Scaling

| Basis   | Questionnaire                               | Scaling                                    |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Meaning | Collection of questions used to gather data | Technique used to measure responses        |
| Purpose | Data collection                             | Measuring attitude, opinion, or perception |
| Output  | Qualitative or quantitative data            | Numerical values for analysis              |
| Example | "Do you use social media?"                  | "Rate your satisfaction from 1 to 5"       |

---

### Conclusion

A **questionnaire** is the overall instrument used to collect data, while **scaling** is a method within the questionnaire that measures the intensity of respondents' attitudes, perceptions, and opinions. In research studies, scaling techniques such as the **Likert Scale** are commonly included in questionnaires to make responses measurable and easier to analyze.

## Likert Scaling

### Definition

**Likert Scaling** is a rating scale used in research to measure a respondent's **attitude, opinion, perception, or level of agreement** toward a statement. It was developed by **Rensis Likert** in 1932.

Instead of giving a simple "Yes" or "No" answer, respondents indicate the degree to which they agree or disagree with a statement.

---

## Standard 5-Point Likert Scale

| Response               | Score |
| ---------------------- | ----- |
| Strongly Agree (SA)    | 5     |
| Agree (A)              | 4     |
| Neutral (N)            | 3     |
| Disagree (D)           | 2     |
| Strongly Disagree (SD) | 1     |

---

**Statement:**

Choose one option:

* ☐ Strongly Agree (5)
* ☐ Agree (4)

* ☐ Disagree (2)
* ☐ Strongly Disagree (1)

### Interpretation

If a student selects:

* **Strongly Agree** , score = 5

Higher scores indicate a more positive attitude toward online learning.

## Types of Likert Scales

### 1. 5-Point Likert Scale

Most widely used.

**Example:**

* Strongly Agree
* Agree
* Neutral
* Disagree
* Strongly Disagree

### 2. 7-Point Likert Scale

**Example:**

* Strongly Agree
* Agree
* Slightly Agree
* Neutral
* Slightly Disagree
* Disagree
* Strongly Disagree

Provides more detailed responses.

### 3. 3-Point Likert Scale

**Example:**

* Agree
* Neutral
* Disagree

Simple and easy to use but provides less detail.

---

## Advantages of Likert Scaling

* Easy to design and administer.
* Simple for respondents to understand.
* Converts attitudes into numerical data.
* Facilitates statistical analysis.
* Widely accepted in academic research.

---

## Limitations of Likert Scaling

* Responses may be influenced by personal bias.
* Some respondents choose the neutral option frequently.
* Different people may interpret scale points differently.

## How to Analyze Likert Scale Data

Let's use a simple example from your research topic:

**Statement:**

> "Online classes improve my learning experience."

### Step 1: Assign Numerical Values

| Response               | Score |
| ---------------------- | ----- |
| Strongly Agree (SA)    | 5     |
| Agree (A)              | 4     |
| Neutral (N)            | 3     |
| Disagree (D)           | 2     |
| Strongly Disagree (SD) | 1     |

---

### Step 2: Collect Responses

Suppose 10 students responded as follows:

| Student | Response | Score |
| ------- | -------- | ----- |
| 1       | SA       | 5     |
| 2       | A        | 4     |
| 3       | SA       | 5     |
| 4       | N        | 3     |
| 5       | A        | 4     |
| 6       | A        | 4     |
| 7       | D        | 2     |
| 8       | SA       | 5     |
| 9       | A        | 4     |
| 10      | SA       | 5     |

---

### Step 3: Calculate Total Score

5+4+5+3+4+4+2+5+4+5=415+4+5+3+4+4+2+5+4+5 = 41
**Total Score = 41**

---

### Step 4: Calculate Mean (Average)

Mean=Total ScoreNumber of Respondents\text{Mean} = \frac{\text{Total Score}}{\text{Number of Respondents}}
Mean=4110\text{Mean} = \frac{41}{10}
Mean=4.1\text{Mean} = 4.1
**Mean Score = 4.1**

---

### Step 5: Interpret the Mean

| Mean Range  | Interpretation    |
| ----------- | ----------------- |
| 4.21 - 5.00 | Strongly Agree    |
| 3.41 - 4.20 | Agree             |
| 2.61 - 3.40 | Neutral           |
| 1.81 - 2.60 | Disagree          |
| 1.00 - 1.80 | Strongly Disagree |

Since **4.1** falls between  **3.41 and 4.20** , respondents **Agree** that online classes improve their learning experience.

---

## Percentage Analysis

Count the responses:

| Response          | Frequency | Percentage |
| ----------------- | --------- | ---------- |
| Strongly Agree    | 4         | 40%        |
| Agree             | 4         | 40%        |
| Neutral           | 1         | 10%        |
| Disagree          | 1         | 10%        |
| Strongly Disagree | 0         | 0%         |

Formula:

Percentage=FrequencyTotal Responses×100\text{Percentage} = \frac{\text{Frequency}}{\text{Total Responses}} \times 100
Example:

410×100=40%\frac \times 100 = 40\%

## Analysis of Multiple Statements

Assume you have 5 questionnaire items:

| Statement               | Mean Score |
| ----------------------- | ---------- |
| Easy access to classes  | 4.2        |
| User-friendly platform  | 4.0        |
| Teacher interaction     | 3.5        |
| Feedback on assignments | 3.8        |
| Overall satisfaction    | 4.1        |

### Grand Mean

4.2+4.0+3.5+3.8+4.15\frac{4.2+4.0+3.5+3.8+4.1}{5}
=19.65= \frac{19.6}{5}
=3.92= 3.92
**Grand Mean = 3.92**

Interpretation: Students generally **Agree** that they are satisfied with online learning.

## Example Write-up for a Research Report

### Analysis and Interpretation

The responses were analyzed using descriptive statistics. A 5-point Likert scale was employed where Strongly Agree = 5, Agree = 4, Neutral = 3, Disagree = 2, and Strongly Disagree = 1. The mean score for the statement "Online classes improve my learning experience" was found to be  **4.1** , indicating that the majority of respondents agreed with the statement. The overall grand mean of **3.92** suggests a positive level of satisfaction with online learning among students.

### Conclusion

For MBA/BBA research projects, the most common way to analyze Likert-scale data is:

1. Convert responses into scores (1-5).
2. Calculate frequencies and percentages.
3. Compute mean scores for each statement.
4. Calculate the grand mean.
5. Interpret the results using predefined mean ranges.

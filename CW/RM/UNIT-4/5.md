## Convenience Sampling

### Definition

**Convenience Sampling** is a **non-probability sampling technique** in which participants are selected because they are easily available, accessible, or willing to participate in the study.

In this method, the researcher chooses subjects based on convenience rather than random selection.

### Example

* Surveying students present in a classroom at the time of data collection.
* Interviewing shoppers in a nearby mall.
* Collecting feedback from people who voluntarily respond to an online form.

### Characteristics

* Easy and quick to conduct.
* Low cost and requires minimal planning.
* Participants are selected based on availability.
* Does not provide every population member an equal chance of selection.

### Advantages

1. Simple and easy to implement.
2. Saves time and resources.
3. Useful for pilot studies and preliminary research.
4. Suitable when a sampling frame is unavailable.

### Disadvantages

1. High risk of sampling bias.
2. Sample may not represent the entire population.
3. Results cannot be generalized confidently.
4. Lower reliability and validity compared to probability sampling methods.

### Use Cases

* Exploratory research.
* Pilot testing of questionnaires.
* Classroom projects with limited time and budget.
* Initial studies before conducting large-scale research.

### Exam-Oriented Answer (5 Marks)

**Convenience Sampling** is a non-probability sampling technique where participants are selected based on their easy availability and accessibility to the researcher. It is widely used because it is quick, economical, and simple to implement. For example, surveying students present in a classroom is a form of convenience sampling. Although it saves time and cost, it may introduce bias and the findings may not accurately represent the entire population. Therefore, it is mainly used in exploratory and pilot studies.

## Purposive (Judgmental) Sampling

### Definition

**Purposive sampling** is a **non-probability sampling technique** in which the researcher  **deliberately selects participants who possess specific characteristics, knowledge, experience, or expertise relevant to the research problem** .

It is also called **Judgmental Sampling** because the researcher uses their **own judgment** to decide who should be included in the sample.

### Simple Meaning

> **“Select the right people for the study because they have the information or characteristics you need.”**

### Example

Suppose a researcher wants to study:

**“Challenges faced by AI researchers in developing Generative AI applications.”**

Instead of randomly selecting 100 people, the researcher may specifically select:

* AI researchers
* ML engineers
* University professors working in AI
* Industry experts in Generative AI

These people are selected because they have  **relevant knowledge and experience** .

### How it works

**Research Problem → Identify required characteristics → Identify suitable participants → Researcher deliberately selects them → Collect data**

### Advantages

1. **Relevant information:** Participants are specifically chosen for their knowledge or experience.
2. **Useful for specialized studies:** Suitable when the target population is small or specialized.
3. **Time and cost effective:** No need to randomly search a large population.
4. **Useful for qualitative research:** Common in interviews, case studies, and expert studies.

### Disadvantages

1. **Researcher bias:** Selection depends on the researcher's judgment.
2. **Limited generalization:** Results may not represent the entire population.
3. **Unknown selection probability:** Every member does not have a known chance of being selected.
4. **Subjective:** Different researchers may select different participants.

### Exam-ready 5-mark answer

**Purposive (Judgmental) Sampling** is a non-probability sampling technique in which the researcher deliberately selects participants based on specific characteristics, knowledge, experience, or expertise relevant to the research study. The selection is based on the researcher's judgment rather than random selection.

**Example:** A researcher studying cybersecurity practices may deliberately select cybersecurity professionals, security analysts, and experienced IT managers as participants.

**Advantages:** Relevant participants, useful for specialized studies, saves time and resources.

**Limitations:** Researcher bias, limited representativeness, and poor generalizability.

**Key point to remember:**
👉 **Purposive Sampling = “I choose participants because they are particularly suitable for my research.”**

# Quota Sampling

### Definition

**Quota sampling** is a **non-probability sampling technique** in which the researcher divides the population into different subgroups and selects a **fixed number (quota)** of participants from each subgroup.

The participants within each subgroup are  **not selected randomly** ; they are usually chosen based on convenience or researcher judgment.

### Simple Example

Suppose a college has **1,000 students** and a researcher wants to survey  **100 students** .

The researcher decides on the following quotas:

| Category        |      Population |         Quota |
| --------------- | --------------: | ------------: |
| 1st Year        |             250 |            25 |
| 2nd Year        |             300 |            30 |
| 3rd Year        |             250 |            25 |
| 4th Year        |             200 |            20 |
| **Total** | **1,000** | **100** |

The researcher then collects responses until each quota is filled.

For example, they may approach easily available students in each year until they have 25, 30, 25, and 20 responses respectively.

### Key Point

> **Quota sampling = Divide into groups + Fix the number required from each group + Select participants non-randomly.**

### Steps

1. Define the target population.
2. Identify important subgroups.
3. Decide the required quota for each subgroup.
4. Select participants using a non-random method.
5. Stop collecting responses when each quota is filled.

### Advantages

* Quick and relatively inexpensive.
* Ensures representation of important subgroups.
* Useful when a complete population list is unavailable.

### Disadvantages

* **Selection bias** can occur because participants are not randomly selected.
* Results may not accurately represent the entire population.
* Generalization to the population is limited.

### Quota vs Stratified Sampling

| Quota Sampling                     | Stratified Sampling               |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Non-probability sampling           | Probability sampling              |
| Fixed quota from each group        | Sample selected from each stratum |
| Participants selected non-randomly | Participants selected randomly    |
| Higher risk of bias                | Lower risk of selection bias      |

**Exam-ready one-liner:**
**Quota sampling is a non-probability sampling technique in which the population is divided into subgroups and a predetermined number of participants is selected from each subgroup without random selection.**

## d) Snowball Sampling

### Definition

**Snowball sampling** is a **non-probability sampling technique** in which existing participants help the researcher **identify or recruit other participants** from their social or professional networks.

It is called  **“snowball” sampling because the sample gradually grows** , just like a snowball rolling downhill.

### Simple Example

Suppose a researcher wants to study **freelance AI developers** in a particular city, but there is no complete list of them.

1. Researcher finds **2 AI developers** who agree to participate.
2. They are asked to refer other AI developers they know.
3. The newly recruited developers refer more people.
4. The process continues until the required sample size is reached.

**2 participants → 5 → 15 → 30 → 50**

### Steps

1. Identify a few initial participants ( **seeds** ).
2. Collect data from them.
3. Ask them to recommend other eligible participants.
4. Contact the referred participants.
5. Continue the referral process until the required sample is obtained.

### When is it useful?

Snowball sampling is particularly useful when the target population is:

* **Difficult to identify**
* **Small or rare**
* **Hidden or hard to reach**
* Connected through social or professional networks

**Examples:**

* Researchers studying rare diseases
* Members of a specialized professional community
* Migrant communities
* People with uncommon experiences

### Advantages

* Easy access to difficult-to-reach participants.
* Relatively inexpensive and time-saving.
* Useful when no sampling frame is available.

### Disadvantages

* High possibility of  **sampling bias** .
* Participants tend to refer people similar to themselves.
* Sample may not represent the entire population.
* Generalization of results is limited.

### Exam-ready definition

> **Snowball sampling is a non-probability sampling technique in which existing participants recruit or refer other eligible participants from their networks, causing the sample to grow progressively.**

**Easy way to remember:**
**“Find a few → ask them to refer → get more → repeat.”**

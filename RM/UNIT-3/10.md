# How to Write Good Items (Statements) in a Questionnaire

Writing good items is crucial because poor questions lead to inaccurate data. A good item should be  **clear, simple, specific, relevant, and unbiased** .

---

## 1. Keep the Item Simple

Use everyday language that respondents can easily understand.

### Good ✅

> "Online shopping saves me time."

### Poor ❌

> "Online shopping enhances consumer purchasing efficiency."

**Exam Tip:** Avoid technical jargon and complex words.

---

## 2. Be Clear and Specific

The respondent should know exactly what is being asked.

### Good ✅

> "Products are delivered within the promised time."

### Poor ❌

> "Delivery service is good."

"Good" can mean different things to different people.

---

## 3. Measure Only One Idea at a Time

Avoid **double-barreled items** (two questions in one).

### Poor ❌

> "The website is easy to use and the delivery service is fast."

A respondent may agree with one part but disagree with the other.

### Good ✅

> "The website is easy to use."
>
> "The delivery service is fast."

---

## 4. Avoid Leading Questions

Do not influence the respondent toward a particular answer.

### Poor ❌

> "Don't you think Amazon provides excellent service?"

### Good ✅

> "How would you rate Amazon's service quality?"

---

## 5. Avoid Ambiguous Words

Words like  *often* ,  *regularly* ,  *many* , *frequently* have different meanings for different people.

### Poor ❌

> "I frequently shop online."

### Good ✅

> "I shop online at least once a week."

---

## 6. Keep Items Relevant to the Research Objective

Every item should help answer the research question.

### Research Topic:

Customer Satisfaction with Online Shopping

### Relevant ✅

> "Customer support resolves my issues quickly."

### Irrelevant ❌

> "I enjoy watching movies."

---

## 7. Avoid Negative Wording (When Possible)

Negative statements may confuse respondents.

### Better ✅

> "Online shopping is convenient."

### Confusing ❌

> "Online shopping is not inconvenient."

---

## 8. Use Balanced Language

Do not use emotional or biased words.

### Poor ❌

> "The amazing delivery service exceeds expectations."

### Good ✅

> "The delivery service meets my expectations."

---

## 9. Ensure Responses Can Vary

The item should not produce the same answer from everyone.

### Poor ❌

> "Internet is useful."

Most people will agree.

### Better ✅

> "I prefer online shopping over visiting physical stores."

---

## 10. Use Multiple Items for One Construct

A single item rarely measures a concept completely.

### Construct: Customer Satisfaction

**Items:**

1. Online shopping saves me time.
2. Products are delivered on time.
3. Online payment is secure.
4. Customer service is helpful.
5. Product quality meets expectations.

Together these items provide a more reliable measure.

---

| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |

# Exam Answer (5 Marks)

**Good questionnaire items should be clear, simple, specific, relevant, and unbiased. Each item should measure only one idea, avoid ambiguity and leading language, and be directly related to the research objective. For example, "Products are delivered on time" is a better item than "The service is good" because it is specific and measurable. Well-written items improve the reliability and validity of research findings.**

### Quick Revision Formula

**C-S-S-R-U**

* **C** = Clear
* **S** = Simple
* **S** = Specific
* **R** = Relevant
* **U** = Unbiased

✅  **Good Item = Clear + Simple + Specific + Relevant + Unbiased** .



# Item Validity and Reliability

When developing a questionnaire, researchers must ensure that the **items (statements)** are both **valid** and  **reliable** .

* **Validity** = Does the item measure what it is supposed to measure?
* **Reliability** = Does the item measure it consistently?

Think of a dartboard:

🎯 **Validity** = Hitting the target.

🎯 **Reliability** = Hitting the same spot repeatedly.

---

# 1. Item Validity

## Definition

Item validity refers to the extent to which a questionnaire item accurately measures the concept it is intended to measure.

### Example

**Research Topic:** Customer Satisfaction

**Item:**

> "Products are delivered on time."

✅ Valid because it measures an aspect of customer satisfaction.

---

### Invalid Item Example

**Research Topic:** Customer Satisfaction

**Item:**

> "I enjoy watching cricket."

❌ Invalid because it has no connection with customer satisfaction.

---

## Types of Validity

### A. Face Validity

Asks whether the item appears to measure the intended concept.

**Example:**

> "Online payment is secure."

At first glance, this seems relevant to online shopping satisfaction.

✅ High Face Validity

---

### B. Content Validity

Checks whether the item covers the important aspects of the construct.

**Customer Satisfaction Construct**

Items:

* Delivery speed
* Product quality
* Payment security
* Customer service

Together they cover the entire concept.

✅ Good Content Validity

---

### C. Construct Validity

Determines whether the item truly measures the theoretical concept.

**Example**

Construct = Trust

Item:

> "I feel safe sharing my payment information on this website."

✅ Measures trust directly.

---

# Item Validity Testing

A common method is  **Item-Total Correlation** .

The correlation of each item is calculated with the total scale score.

### Rule

* Correlation > 0.30 → Valid Item ✅
* Correlation < 0.30 → Weak/Invalid Item ❌

### Example

| Item               | Correlation |
| ------------------ | ----------- |
| Delivery Speed     | 0.72        |
| Product Quality    | 0.65        |
| Payment Security   | 0.58        |
| Cricket Preference | 0.10        |

The "Cricket Preference" item should be removed.

---

# 2. Item Reliability

## Definition

Reliability refers to the consistency of an item or scale.

If respondents answer similarly under similar conditions, the item is reliable.

---

## Example

**Item:**

> "Online shopping saves time."

If respondents consistently provide similar responses over repeated measurements, the item is reliable.

---

## Unreliable Item Example

> "Online shopping is somewhat fairly convenient in certain situations."

The statement is vague and may be interpreted differently.

❌ Low Reliability

---

# Methods of Reliability Testing

## A. Test-Retest Reliability

The same questionnaire is given twice.

### Example

Survey students today and again after two weeks.

If responses are similar:

✅ High Reliability

---

## B. Internal Consistency Reliability

Checks whether all items measuring the same construct are related.

Usually measured using **Cronbach's Alpha (α).**

---

# Cronbach's Alpha

Most common reliability measure in research.

### Interpretation

| Alpha Value | Reliability  |
| ----------- | ------------ |
| ≥ 0.90     | Excellent    |
| 0.80 - 0.89 | Very Good    |
| 0.70 - 0.79 | Acceptable   |
| 0.60 - 0.69 | Questionable |
| < 0.60      | Poor         |

---

### Example

Customer Satisfaction Scale:

| Item                        |
| --------------------------- |
| Delivery is timely          |
| Products are reliable       |
| Website is easy to use      |
| Customer support is helpful |

Cronbach's Alpha = 0.84

✅ Reliable Scale

---

# Difference Between Validity and Reliability

| Basis    | Validity                                | Reliability                                |
| -------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Meaning  | Measures the right thing                | Measures consistently                      |
| Focus    | Accuracy                                | Consistency                                |
| Question | "Am I measuring the correct concept?"   | "Am I getting the same result repeatedly?" |
| Example  | Satisfaction item measures satisfaction | Satisfaction item gives stable responses   |

---

# Practical Example

### Research Topic: Online Shopping Satisfaction

### Item

> "Products are delivered on time."

### Validity Check

* Related to customer satisfaction? ✅ Yes
* Correlation > 0.30? ✅ Yes

Therefore, the item is  **valid** .

### Reliability Check

* Gives consistent responses across respondents? ✅
* Contributes to Cronbach's Alpha > 0.70? ✅

Therefore, the item is  **reliable** .

# Exam Answer (5 Marks)

**Item validity refers to the degree to which a questionnaire item measures what it is intended to measure. It ensures the accuracy and relevance of the item. Item reliability refers to the consistency of responses obtained from an item across different measurements. Validity is commonly assessed through face validity, content validity, and construct validity, while reliability is often measured using Cronbach's Alpha and test-retest methods. A good research item should be both valid and reliable.**

## Quick Revision

✅ **Validity = Accuracy (Right Measurement)**

✅ **Reliability = Consistency (Same Measurement Repeatedly)**

### Research Design (Simple Definition)

**Research Design** is the overall plan or blueprint of a research study. It explains **how the researcher will collect, analyze, and interpret data** to answer the research question.

### Simple Example

**Research Topic:** Impact of Social Media on Students' Academic Performance

**Research Design:**

* Research Method: Survey
* Participants: 100 college students
* Data Collection: Questionnaire
* Data Analysis: Statistical analysis of responses

Here, the **research design** is the structured plan that guides the researcher from data collection to data analysis.

### Student-Friendly Definition

> **Research Design is a systematic plan that guides a researcher in conducting a study and answering the research question effectively and accurately.**

### Real-Life Example

Suppose a researcher wants to find out whether  **online learning improves student performance** .

The researcher decides to:

1. Select 50 students.
2. Conduct online classes for one semester.
3. Collect students' exam scores.
4. Compare results before and after online learning.

This complete strategy is called the  **research design** .

**In short:** Research Design = **The roadmap of research that shows what data will be collected, from whom, and how it will be analyzed.**


### Research Design - Qualitative

#### Simple Definition

**Qualitative Research Design** is a research approach used to understand people's experiences, opinions, behaviors, feelings, and perceptions in depth. It focuses on collecting **non-numerical data** such as interviews, observations, and discussions.

> **Qualitative Research Design is a method of research that explores and understands human experiences, thoughts, and behaviors through detailed and descriptive data.**

---

### Example

**Research Topic:** Students' Experiences with Online Learning

**Qualitative Research Design:**

* Method: Interviews
* Participants: 20 college students
* Data Collection: One-to-one interviews
* Data Analysis: Identifying common themes and opinions

**Sample Findings:**

* Students appreciated the flexibility of online classes.
* Some students faced internet connectivity issues.
* Many students felt less engaged compared to classroom learning.

Since the study focuses on students' **opinions and experiences** rather than numerical data, it uses a  **qualitative research design** .

---

### Student-Friendly Explanation

Suppose a researcher wants to know:

**"How do students feel about using social media for learning?"**

The researcher:

1. Interviews students.
2. Records their responses.
3. Analyzes their views and experiences.
4. Identifies common patterns and themes.

This process represents a **qualitative research design** because it seeks detailed understanding rather than statistical results.

---

### Short Academic Definition

> **A qualitative research design is a systematic approach used to explore and interpret individuals' experiences, perceptions, and behaviors through detailed, non-numerical data collected from interviews, observations, and discussions.**
>



### Research Design - Quantitative Research

#### Simple Definition

**Quantitative Research Design** is a research approach that focuses on collecting and analyzing **numerical data** to identify patterns, relationships, or trends. It uses statistical methods to answer research questions objectively.

> **Quantitative Research Design is a systematic method of collecting and analyzing numerical data to measure variables and test hypotheses.**

---

### Example

**Research Topic:** Effect of Social Media Usage on Students' Academic Performance

**Quantitative Research Design:**

* Participants: 100 college students
* Data Collection: Structured questionnaire
* Data Type: Numerical data (hours spent on social media, exam scores)
* Data Analysis: Statistical techniques such as percentages, averages, and correlation analysis

**Sample Findings:**

* Students who use social media for more than 4 hours daily scored an average of 65%.
* Students who use social media for less than 2 hours daily scored an average of 78%.

Since the study uses  **numbers and statistical analysis** , it follows a  **quantitative research design** .

---

### Student-Friendly Explanation

Suppose a researcher wants to answer the question:

**"Does online learning improve students' academic performance?"**

The researcher:

1. Selects 200 students.
2. Collects their examination scores.
3. Records attendance data.
4. Analyzes the data using statistical methods.
5. Draws conclusions based on numerical results.

This is an example of a **quantitative research design** because the study relies on measurable data and statistical analysis.

---

### Short Academic Definition

> **Quantitative research design is a structured research approach that involves collecting numerical data and applying statistical techniques to examine relationships, test hypotheses, and draw objective conclusions.**
>

### Difference Between Qualitative and Quantitative Research Design (with Scenario)

The easiest way to understand the difference is to remember:

* **Qualitative = "Why and How?"** (opinions, experiences, feelings)
* **Quantitative = "How many and How much?"** (numbers, measurements, statistics)

---

## Scenario: Online Learning and Students

Imagine a researcher wants to study:

**"How does online learning affect students?"**

### 1. Qualitative Research Design

The researcher wants to understand students'  **experiences and feelings** .

**What the researcher does:**

* Interviews 20 students.
* Asks open-ended questions.
* Records their responses.
* Identifies common themes.

**Questions asked:**

* How do you feel about online learning?
* What challenges do you face?
* What benefits do you experience?

**Sample Responses:**

* "I enjoy learning from home."
* "Internet issues affect my studies."
* "I feel less connected to my classmates."

**Conclusion:**
The researcher learns **why** students like or dislike online learning.

✅ Focus: Experiences, opinions, and perceptions.

---

### 2. Quantitative Research Design

The researcher wants numerical evidence about online learning.

**What the researcher does:**

* Surveys 200 students.
* Collects exam scores and attendance records.
* Uses statistical analysis.

**Questions asked:**

* How many hours do you spend in online classes?
* What is your average exam score?

**Sample Results:**

* 75% of students attend online classes regularly.
* Average score increased from 68% to 78%.
* 60% of students reported improved performance.

**Conclusion:**
The researcher determines **how much** online learning affects academic performance.

✅ Focus: Numbers, percentages, and measurements.

---

## Quick Comparison

| Aspect      | Qualitative Research Design            | Quantitative Research Design         |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Purpose     | Understand experiences and opinions    | Measure facts and relationships      |
| Data Type   | Words, descriptions                    | Numbers, statistics                  |
| Methods     | Interviews, observations, focus groups | Surveys, questionnaires, experiments |
| Sample Size | Usually small                          | Usually large                        |
| Answers     | Why? How?                              | How many? How much?                  |
| Output      | Themes and insights                    | Statistical results                  |

---

### Simple One-Line Example

**Research Question:** "Do students like online learning?"

* **Qualitative:** Interview students and ask *why* they like or dislike it.
* **Quantitative:** Survey 500 students and calculate the percentage who like it.

### Easy Way to Remember

> **Qualitative research tells the story behind the data, while Quantitative research provides the numbers behind the story.**

# 1. Qualitative Research Design

### Definition

Qualitative Research Design is used to understand people's  **experiences, opinions, feelings, and perceptions** . It collects **non-numerical data** such as words and descriptions.

### Characteristics

* Focuses on "Why?" and "How?"
* Uses interviews and observations
* Small sample size
* Produces descriptive findings

* Interview 20 students
* Ask questions about their experiences

**Sample Findings:**

* Students enjoy learning from home.
* Some face internet connectivity problems.
* Many miss face-to-face interaction.

### Conclusion

Qualitative research helps understand  **students' feelings and experiences** .

# 2. Quantitative Research Design

### Definition

Quantitative Research Design is used to collect and analyze  **numerical data** . It helps measure variables and test relationships using statistics.

### Characteristics

* Focuses on "How many?" and "How much?"
* Uses surveys and questionnaires
* Large sample size
* Produces statistical results

### Example

**Topic:** Effect of Online Learning on Academic Performance

**Method:**

* Survey 200 students
* Collect exam scores

**Sample Findings:**

* Average score increased from 70% to 80%.
* 75% of students preferred online learning.

### Conclusion

Quantitative research provides **numerical evidence** about the impact of online learning.

# Easy Exam Answer (3 Marks)

**Qualitative Research Design** focuses on understanding people's experiences, feelings, and opinions through non-numerical data such as interviews and observations.

**Quantitative Research Design** focuses on collecting and analyzing numerical data through surveys and statistical methods to measure relationships and trends.

**Example:** If a researcher interviews students about their online learning experiences, it is qualitative research. If the researcher surveys 200 students and analyzes their marks statistically, it is quantitative research.

### Memory Trick

✅ **Qualitative = Quality of opinions (Words)**

✅ **Quantitative = Quantity of data (Numbers)**

> **Qualitative tells the story, while Quantitative provides the numbers.**
